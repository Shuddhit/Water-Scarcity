Digital GIS layer concept

The project can be understood as multiple spatial layers that are combined to identify areas requiring environmental attention.

01 · Groundwater layer

Maps groundwater-level or extraction indicators and identifies locations where aquifer pressure needs attention.

Aquifer / groundwater

02 · Urban supply layer

Represents supply reliability, service gaps, demand pressure and distribution-system performance.

Water supply

03 · Surface-water layer

Considers lakes, reservoirs, wetlands, catchments and water-body resilience as environmental assets.

Lakes / wetlands

04 · Remote-sensing layer

Satellite-derived indicators such as NDWI can help analyse surface-water presence and change when appropriate imagery and dates are documented.

NDWI / satellite

05 · Climate layer

Heat exposure and rainfall variability can be added as contextual stressors affecting urban water demand and resilience.

Climate pressure

06 · Composite hotspot

Normalised indicators can be weighted into a transparent composite score and classified into four hotspot categories.

Multi-criteria index

Methodology · from data to map

A clear workflow makes the website suitable for an Environmental Studies presentation and gives the project a reproducible structure.

01

Collect

Gather groundwater, water-supply, surface-water and climate indicators from documented datasets.

02

Clean

Check units, dates, missing values, geographic boundaries and duplicate records.

03

Normalize

Convert indicators to a comparable 0–100 pressure scale using a documented method.

04

Weight

Assign transparent weights based on the study objective and test sensitivity.

05

Map

Join the results to city, ward or other geographic units and visualize hotspot classes.

06

Respond

Translate hotspots into practical environmental interventions and monitoring priorities.

Academic data note: The interactive map and interface are real, but the city hotspot scores in this submission-ready prototype are illustrative demonstration values. They should not be presented as official government ratings. A research-grade version should attach every numerical input to a source, dataset year/date, geographic unit and reproducible calculation.

Environmental interventions

Hotspot mapping becomes useful when it leads to practical environmental action.

Restore aquifersPromote rainwater harvesting and protect recharge zones where suitable.
Close the loopExpand safe treatment and reuse of wastewater for suitable non-potable uses.
Protect water bodiesRestore lakes, wetlands and catchments that strengthen urban water resilience.

Project conclusion

Digital mapping provides a visual bridge between environmental data and decision-making. By combining groundwater pressure, urban supply conditions, surface-water resilience, climate context and spatial analysis, JalDrishti demonstrates how Indian cities can be compared through a transparent hotspot framework. The final goal is not simply to colour a map, but to identify where water stress may require monitoring, conservation, recharge, reuse, infrastructure improvement and protection of urban water ecosystems.
